# What is what: the three filing packages explained

Inventor and applicant: Ravi Kiran Raju Yerra, Celina, Texas. Title: Screen-Aware, Read-Only Task Guidance with On-Device Redaction and Tenant-Governed Data Egress. Entity status: small entity. Filing: pro se through USPTO Patent Center.

## Which package to file

There are three packages in patent-package/final. They share the same specification, claims, abstract and drawings; they differ only in what is filed and when.

1. provisional. File this now to secure the filing date at the lowest cost (small entity provisional fee 130 USD as recorded; verify at filing). It is never examined and expires in twelve months. Use it when you want the date locked immediately, for example because the product is already public, and you want up to twelve months before paying the nonprovisional fees.
2. utility. File this if you skip the provisional and file the full nonprovisional application directly (970 USD small entity as recorded for 20 claims with 4 independent claims, of which 240 USD is the one excess-independent-claim fee; verify at filing). The filing date of this application is the effective date of every claim. It is the right choice when you are ready to start examination now and do not need the twelve-month interval.
3. utility-with-priority. File this within twelve months after the provisional, and only then. It is the utility package with a benefit claim to the provisional added, so that every claim keeps the provisional's earlier date. The only two values entered by hand are the provisional's application number and filing date, copied from the provisional filing receipt.

Use either route 1 then 3, or route 2 alone. Never file both utility packages.

## Provisional versus utility in one paragraph

A provisional application is a date-stamp: it records what you disclosed on a given day, costs little, has no claims requirement, no declaration, no examination and no publication, and lapses after twelve months. A nonprovisional utility application is the real application: it has formal claims, a signed declaration, full fees, an IDS, examination by an examiner, publication at eighteen months, and it can issue as a patent. A provisional filed first does not delay anything the applicant controls; it buys up to twelve months to refine the claims, decide on foreign filing and spread the cost, at the price of filing twice and of a patent term that still runs from the nonprovisional filing date. If you are certain of the claims and want examination to start now, the standalone utility application saves one filing; if you want the date secured today and time to decide, the provisional followed by the utility-with-priority application is the usual route for an individual inventor.

## The 2026-10-27 additions are in the one application, not in a continuation-in-part

On 2026-10-27 the product gained a multi-task project engine (a goal planned once into a dependency-ordered map of tasks with screen-visible done-states, each task's steps expanded just in time from a project-scoped forward-learnings ledger, with a deterministic breakdown gate, mid-project task injection and project reports) and a watch-loop tolerance for transient unreadable frames. An earlier review drafted these as candidates for a continuation-in-part, on the assumption that a parent application was already on file. That assumption does not hold: nothing in this package has been filed. A continuation-in-part exists to add new matter to an application that already has a filing date, and the price of a continuation-in-part is that the new matter gets only its own later date while every claim that depends on it must be traced to the later disclosure. Since there is no filed parent, the right course is simply to put the new embodiments into the one specification, draw them (FIG. 10 to FIG. 13) and regenerate all three packages from the single sources. The lead set then claims the project engine as claims 19 and 20 (the project-engine method and its deterministic breakdown gate); the other 2026-10-27 additions (the watch-loop tolerance and the remaining project-engine claims) were trimmed from the lead set on 2026-10-28 to hold the 20-claim count and are held for a continuation or divisional from the same deposited disclosure (see claims.md Appendix A and Appendix B). Every claim then has the same effective filing date, the one on which the chosen package is filed, with no new-matter question and no priority split. The provisional, if that route is taken, carries the full disclosure, so the utility-with-priority application can claim the provisional's date for the new claims too. The three packages keep their meaning: the provisional is the date-stamp with the full disclosure and the claims as support; the utility is the formal standalone application; the utility-with-priority is the utility plus the 35 U.S.C. 119(e) benefit claim with only the provisional number and date entered by hand.

## Decisions that remain with the inventor or a practitioner

1. Route: provisional first (packages 1 then 3) or the standalone utility application (package 2).
2. Claim count. The set is built with 20 claims and 4 independent claims, which costs one excess-independent-claim fee of 240 USD as recorded (for the fourth independent claim, claim 19, the project-engine method, kept for its value). A practitioner may drop claim 19 and its dependent claim 20 to reach 18 claims and 3 independent claims and remove that fee, holding the project engine for a continuation or divisional; the fee worksheet shows both figures.
3. Whether to add any Appendix A claim at filing (further fees, probable restriction) or hold them for a continuation or divisional.
4. Whether to check the nonpublication request on the ADS (the packages leave it unchecked).
5. The must-pull references in ids-and-references.md section 6: read them and list or omit each before the IDS is final, or supplement within three months.
6. For claims that recite embodiments described in the specification as "in some embodiments" or "in one embodiment" (lock release with re-authentication, claim 15; the instruction-shaping rule set limb of claim 14; the second alternative of claim 6; the completion-time ledger write and the final limb that the project end-state is shown satisfied, claim 19), the inventor confirms that he conceived each before signing the declaration; the specification describes them, and the declaration covers every claimed invention. Appendix B of claims.md states, per claim, which legs are shipped and which are specification-supported only.
7. Whether to retain a registered patent practitioner for examination. Filing pro se is permitted; prosecution (responding to Office actions, amending claims) is where practitioner help has the greatest effect.
