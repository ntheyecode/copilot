# Information Disclosure Statement (37 CFR 1.97 and 1.98): content and reference list

## 1. Purpose and timing

An Information Disclosure Statement (IDS) lists the prior art and other information known to the applicant that may be material to patentability (37 CFR 1.56). It is filed on form PTO/SB/08a (U.S. patents and U.S. patent application publications) and PTO/SB/08b (foreign patent documents and non-patent literature), or through the Patent Center IDS web form, which produces the same listing. File it in the same Patent Center submission as the application. An IDS filed within three months of the filing date, or before the first Office action on the merits, needs no fee and no statement (37 CFR 1.97(b)). An IDS filed later needs the fee under 37 CFR 1.17(p) or a certification under 37 CFR 1.97(e).

Listing a reference is not an admission that it is prior art or that it is material. When in doubt, list it. Items dated 2025 or 2026 are prior art only if their dates precede the effective filing date of this application; they are listed regardless, with their dates, so that the examiner has them.

## 7. Supplemental IDS

If any must-pull item proves material after the application is filed, file a supplemental IDS. Within three months of filing, or before the first Office action, no fee or statement is needed. After that, the 37 CFR 1.97(e) statement or the 37 CFR 1.17(p) fee applies, and after a final action or allowance the requirements tighten further; the duty of disclosure continues until the patent issues.
