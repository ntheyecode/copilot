# Filing instructions: nonprovisional utility application claiming the benefit of the provisional

<!-- intro-start -->

This package files the nonprovisional utility application under 35 U.S.C. 111(a) that claims the benefit of the provisional application filed from patent-package/final/provisional. It is identical to the standalone utility package except for the benefit claim. Every claim that is supported by the provisional's specification receives the provisional's filing date as its effective filing date; the specification in both packages is the same text, so every claim is supported.

Exactly two values are entered by hand in this package, both copied from the provisional application's filing receipt: the provisional application number (format 63/xxx,xxx) and the provisional filing date. They appear in two places: the first paragraph of the specification ("Cross-Reference to Related Applications", in specification.md and in application-body.md and their DOCX files) and the Domestic Benefit section of the ADS (application-data-sheet.md section 7). Everything else is already filled.

The deadline is absolute: this application must be filed within twelve months of the provisional filing date (35 U.S.C. 119(e)(1)). Mark that date now. If the provisional expires unfiled, the provisional's date is lost and any public disclosure in the meantime becomes prior art abroad and, after one year, in the United States as well.

DOCX versus PDF. The USPTO charges a non-DOCX surcharge (37 CFR 1.16(u); 172 USD small entity as recorded, verify current USPTO fee schedule at filing) when the specification, claims or abstract of a nonprovisional application are filed in a format other than DOCX. Upload the application body as DOCX to avoid it. If Patent Center flags a formatting warning, read it; warnings about fonts or tables are informational, errors must be corrected in Word and the file re-uploaded. After filing, download the USPTO-rendered PDF of the application from Patent Center and keep it with the DOCX. The drawings, declaration and IDS are filed as PDF; no surcharge applies to them.

## 6. After submission

1. Filing Receipt. The USPTO issues the official Filing Receipt some weeks after submission; it appears in Patent Center and is sent to the correspondence address. Check it line by line against the EAR and the ADS: inventor name, title, application number, confirmation number, filing date, entity status (should read small), total claims 20, independent claims 4, drawing sheets 13. Request correction in Patent Center if anything is wrong; errors in the Filing Receipt are corrected without fee when the USPTO made them.
2. Foreign filing license. The Filing Receipt states whether a foreign filing license under 35 U.S.C. 184 is granted. Do not file in another country or under the PCT before the license is granted or six months have passed from the filing date.
3. Notices. If something was missing or defective, a Notice to File Missing Parts or a Notice to File Corrected Application Papers sets a two-month period to fix it; respond within the period. With this package complete, none is expected.
4. Correspondence. Office actions and notices arrive in Patent Center and, with e-Office Action opted in, by email notification. Each sets a response deadline; most Office actions allow three months, extendable to six with extension fees. Missing a deadline abandons the application.
5. Publication. The application publishes about 18 months after the filing date unless a nonpublication request was made. Publication starts provisional rights (35 U.S.C. 154(d)).
6. Examination. A first Office action typically arrives one to three years after filing, depending on the art unit. The prioritized examination program (Track One, 37 CFR 1.102(e)) is available for an additional fee if a faster first action is wanted; it can be requested at filing or with a request for continued examination.

## 8. The benefit claim: the two values to enter and the deadlines

<!-- benefit-start -->

This package already claims the benefit of the provisional application. Complete the benefit claim as follows; these are the only two values entered by hand anywhere in this package.

1. Open the provisional application's filing receipt (the Electronic Acknowledgement Receipt from the day of submission, or the official Filing Receipt in Patent Center). Copy the application number (format 63/xxx,xxx) and the filing date (the date the provisional was filed, not the date the receipt was printed).
2. Specification. In editable/application-body.docx (and, to keep the masters consistent, in specification.md, application-body.md and editable/specification.docx) replace "[ENTER PROVISIONAL APPLICATION NUMBER FROM FILING RECEIPT]" with the number and "[ENTER PROVISIONAL FILING DATE]" with the date written out (for example "January 15, 2027"), so that the first paragraph reads: "This application claims the benefit of U.S. Provisional Application No. 63/xxx,xxx, filed Month D, YYYY." Search the document for "[ENTER" afterwards to confirm no marker remains.
3. ADS. In the web ADS section "Domestic Benefit/National Stage Information" enter: Prior application status "Pending"; Application number (this application) left blank; Continuity type "Claims benefit of provisional application" (35 U.S.C. 119(e)); Prior application number: the provisional number; Filing date: the provisional filing date in YYYY-MM-DD form. application-data-sheet.md section 7 carries the same two markers.
4. Deadlines, both hard:
   The nonprovisional application must be filed no later than twelve months after the provisional filing date (35 U.S.C. 119(e)(1)). If the last day is a Saturday, Sunday or federal holiday the period runs to the next business day, but do not plan on it. A provisional cannot be extended; if the twelve months are missed by up to two months, only a petition under 37 CFR 1.78(b) with a fee and a statement of unintentional delay can restore the benefit.
   The benefit claim must be presented in the ADS within the later of four months from the filing date of this application or sixteen months from the provisional filing date (37 CFR 1.78(a)(4)). Entering it in the ADS at filing satisfies this; a claim added later needs a petition under 37 CFR 1.78(c) with a fee.
5. The provisional and this application both name Ravi Kiran Raju Yerra as sole inventor, which satisfies the common-inventor requirement of 35 U.S.C. 119(e).
6. After filing, check the Filing Receipt: it must list the provisional application under "Domestic Priority data as claimed by applicant" with the correct number and date. If it does not, file a corrected ADS within the period in item 4 and request a corrected Filing Receipt.
7. The provisional application itself requires nothing further. It is never examined and goes abandoned automatically at twelve months; its contents stay confidential unless this application, which refers to it, is published, after which the provisional's file becomes available to the public.

## 9. Mistakes to avoid

1. Uploading claims.docx with Appendix A and Appendix B still attached. Upload editable/application-body.docx, which omits both, or delete the appendices first.
2. Leaving the review line in the page header of the filed DOCX.
3. Filing the application body as PDF (surcharge).
4. Selecting the wrong entity status (micro is not available; large pays more than double).
5. Signing the declaration before reading the final application, or having anyone other than the inventor sign it.
6. Omitting the signed declaration (surcharge and a notice to file it).
7. Entering the inventor name differently in the ADS and the declaration. It is "Ravi Kiran Raju" (given) and "Yerra" (family) in both.
8. Forgetting to download the Electronic Acknowledgement Receipt and, later, the Filing Receipt.
9. Letting the twelve-month foreign filing window pass without a decision.
10. Leaving either "[ENTER ...]" marker in the filed specification or omitting the benefit claim from the ADS.
