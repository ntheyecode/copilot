Scope: a web search (search-engine queries plus opening the record pages of the hits that mattered) run on 2026-10-27 for the subject matter added to the application that day (specification paragraphs [0090] to [0112], FIG. 10 to FIG. 13, claims 10 and 22 to 29). ## 1. Two-tier, just-in-time decomposition for a human performer (claims 22, 28, 29)

| Reference | What it teaches | How it differs |
|---|---|---|
| ADaPT, arXiv 2311.05772 (Prasad et al., 2023, v2 2024) | A planner language model decomposes a sub-task recursively only when an executor language model fails at it, so decomposition depth follows difficulty. | The trigger is executor failure, not a task becoming actionable in a dependency order; the performer is a language-model agent; there is no up-front map carrying a per-task done-state and a project end-state. |
| US 2024/0330654 A1 (State Farm, published 2024-10-03) | A chatbot splits a human user's objective into discrete steps with personalized instructions and target dates, tracks progress and returns updated steps. | One tier: steps and their instructions are generated together; no deferral of step-level detail until a task is actionable; no screen-visible done-state, no verification from a frame. |
| US 12,645,890 B2 (ASAPP, granted 2026-06-02) | A task-level prompt selects a subtask template, a subtask-level prompt selects the next browser action, and after each step the system decides subtask-done, task-done or continue. | Two prompt tiers exist, but the system executes the actions in the browser itself; there is no human performer, no up-front map with done-states and an end-state, and expansion is per action rather than per actionable task. |
| HTN plus language-model method learning, arXiv 2511.12901 (2025; snippet only) | A symbolic hierarchical-task-network planner calls a language model only when no method matches a compound task, with a verifier on each decomposition. | On-demand decomposition for an automated planner, not actionability-triggered expansion for a guided human. |

Novelty delta: no reference found combines (a) an upper tier planned once that carries a screen-visible done-state per task and a verifiable project end-state, (b) step-level expansion deferred until the task becomes actionable in the dependency graph, (c) a human performer guided one instruction at a time with each step verified from a redacted frame, and (d) a project-scoped ledger that feeds later expansions. The obviousness risk is a combination of State Farm (human objective split into tracked steps) with ADaPT or ASAPP (two-level or deferred decomposition), which is why the done-state and end-state contract, the actionability trigger and the ledger are all recited in the independent claims. Risk grade: moderate; the claim survives only as the full combination.

## 2. Screen or vision-verified step guidance (confirmation check for claims 1, 11, 17)

| Reference | What it teaches | How it differs |
|---|---|---|
| US 12,567,270 B1 (Squint Inc., filed 2025-08-28, granted 2026-03-03) | In a guided workflow, a user-initiated verification sends an image plus a text description of the desired state to a grounded computer-vision model and a vision-language model driven by a chain-of-thought controller that re-prompts up to an iteration threshold, then reports whether the target is in the desired state; backup tools are used when the default is unavailable or slow. | Aimed at physical equipment (displays mentioned as possible targets); verification is user-initiated rather than continuously sampled; no on-device redaction, no tenant egress policy, no consecutive-frame tolerance, and escalation is availability-driven rather than capability-driven. New since the earlier searches; added to the IDS as entry 34. |
| STEVE step-verification pipeline (2025; snippet only) | A vision model labels each agent step correct or not by comparing screens before and after the action. | Offline training of autonomous agents, not live guidance of a human. |
| screen.vision (open-source project; snippet only; date needed) | Captures the screen, gives step-by-step instructions and verifies completion by comparing before and after screen states with separate models. | No redaction, egress policy or task-map tiering; close to the generic loop, so its first-commit date must be established. |
| JARVIS AR task guidance, arXiv 2604.10108 (2026; snippet only) | Augmented-reality guidance for physical tasks with user-triggered vision-language state checks. | Physical tasks, user-triggered, no screen or redaction. |

Novelty delta: nothing found displaces the existing position, but the 2026 Squint grant and the open-source project confirm that "a vision model verifies a guided step from an image" is now a crowded generic concept. The anchor claims continue to rest on the on-device redacted frame that is at once the model input, the verification artifact and the sole evidence, on the plan-embedded visible-when-done condition and on the tenant policy, not on vision verification as such. Risk grade for the generic concept: high; the differentiators are what carry the claims.

## 3. Project-scoped forward-learnings ledger (claims 22, 25, 28, 29)

| Reference | What it teaches | How it differs |
|---|---|---|
| Reflexion, arXiv 2303.11366 (Shinn et al., 2023) | Verbal self-reflections on task feedback are stored in an episodic memory buffer and prepended to later trials. | Memory serves retries of the same task, not facts carried to different sibling tasks of one project; no deduplication or bound described. |
| Verifiable Memory, arXiv 2608.03137 (2026; snippet only) | Persistent memory entries carry source and status, separate from episodic history, with verifiers deciding what persists. | General agent memory management; not project-scoped injection of verified facts into the planning prompt of the next task of a human project; dated 2026, so its status as prior art depends on the filing date. |
| Working memory for language-model agents, arXiv 2312.17259 (2023; snippet only) | Argues for an episodic buffer so working memory carries between tasks instead of each session being isolated. | Architectural proposal without a bounded, deduplicated ledger of verified current facts scoped to a project. |
| gptme lesson system (blog; snippet only; undated) | Keyword-matched lessons injected into the system prompt, bounded by context budget. | Cross-session agent lessons, not facts learned during earlier tasks of the same human project; no deduplication described. |

Novelty delta: the closest art is same-task retry memory and generic agent memory stores; none scopes learnings to a project, deduplicates and bounds them, and injects them as verified current facts into the planning of later sibling tasks performed by a human. The risk is that "store lessons, inject into later prompts" reads as routine, which is why claim 25 ties the ledger write to the task's verified completion and to the recorded discrepancy indication, and why the independent claims tie the ledger to the task map and its done-states. Risk grade: moderate.

## 4. Deterministic granularity gates on a model-generated decomposition (claims 23, 29)

| Reference | What it teaches | How it differs |
|---|---|---|
| VeriMAP, arXiv 2510.17109 (Xu et al., 2025-10-20) | The planner emits per-subtask verification functions, aggregated by logical AND, with re-planning on failure. | Verifies subtask outputs at run time, not the structural granularity of the decomposition (duplicates, dependency resolution, acyclicity, presence of a done-state). |
| GNNVerifier, arXiv 2603.14730 (Hao et al., 2026-03-16) | A plan is a directed graph; a graph neural network scores the structure and flags type mismatches, missing intermediates and broken dependencies. | A learned verifier, not model-free deterministic rules; no duplicate collapse, cycle check or minimum-count rule described. |
| PtcRunner.Plan validator (HexDocs ptc_runner v0.7.0; snippet only) | Language-model plans are checked for duplicate task identifiers, references to nonexistent agents, and cycles by topological sort. | Generic orchestration-library checks; no requirement of a goal plus a verifiable done-state per sub-task, no minimum-two rule, no over-split-is-error versus under-split-is-advisory asymmetry. |
| OrchBench, arXiv 2607.25656 (2026; snippet only) | Subtask graphs are checked for exact and near-duplicate descriptions at a high normalized text-similarity threshold. | Benchmark evaluation of orchestration plans, not a gate in a human-guidance planner. |

Novelty delta: each individual check (duplicate identifiers, cycle detection, dependency resolution, near-duplicate labels) is known in open-source validators and benchmarks, so claim 23 cannot rest on any one of them. The claimable delta is the bundle applied to a human task map: at least two distinct real tasks, each requiring both an aim and a screen-visible done-state, duplicates collapsed by normalized label before counting, over-splitting rejected while under-splitting is only advisory, and an end-state required; no single reference found teaches that bundle. Risk grade: moderate as the bundle, high if any check were claimed alone.

## 5. Mid-project insertion into a dependency graph with token-set dedupe (claim 24)

| Reference | What it teaches | How it differs |
|---|---|---|
| US 8,887,163 B2 (eBay, granted 2014-11-11) | Newly discovered prerequisites are topologically sorted and registered least-dependent first; static cycles raise an exception; dynamic cycles mark dependents failed and execution continues. | Machine job scheduling; no text-similarity dedupe, no placement before a named blocker inheriting its upstream dependencies or at the frontier; a cycle causes an exception or a failure mark rather than append-with-notice. |
| US 12,248,810 B2 (IBM, granted 2025-03-11) | A new task node is woven into a directed acyclic graph by matching its inputs to upstream outputs; an inconsistency halts insertion and notifies the task manager. | The new node is always a leaf; duplicates are found by input-output rule matching, not token-set similarity; no cycle probe; failure halts rather than degrading to append-with-notice. |
| US 7,949,610 B2 (IBM, granted 2011-05-24) | A task is inserted immediately after its prerequisite (or the prerequisite immediately before an existing task), with an existence check and merging of dependencies that share a common task. | Exact-match existence check only; no Jaccard dedupe; no cycle handling; placement relative to one prerequisite, not the latest of several nor the frontier. |
| Jaccard duplicate-task detection in project management | No usable hit (results were string-matching and code-clone art). | |

Novelty delta: the graph mechanics (topological sort, cycle detection, place-after-prerequisite, merge) are mature in scheduling and project-plan patents, so claim 24 cannot rest on them. The delta is the combination of token-set dedupe against existing nodes, three-way dependency-aware placement (before a named blocker inheriting its upstream dependencies, after the latest named dependency, or at the frontier), and a cycle probe that degrades to append-with-notice rather than refusing the user, inside a live human-guided project. Risk grade: moderate; claim 24 is kept dependent.

## 6. Consecutive-failure tolerance in a frame-sampled vision loop (claim 10)

| Reference | What it teaches | How it differs |
|---|---|---|
| AutoMix, arXiv 2310.12963 (Madaan et al., 2023 to 2025; snippet only) | A small model answers, self-verifies by entailment, and a meta-verifier decides whether to route the query to a larger model. | Per-query escalation on verified unreliability; no per-session consecutive streak, no withholding of the stronger model within a tolerance, no reset on a readable frame. |
| FrugalGPT, arXiv 2305.05176 (Chen et al., 2023; snippet only) | A cascade queries models in sequence and stops when a scorer passes a quality threshold. | Per-query cost cascade; no temporal streak logic and no incident suppression. |
| RouteLLM, arXiv 2406.18665 (Ong et al., 2024; snippet only) | A trained router sends each query to one model before generation. | Single-shot routing, not failure-driven escalation. |
| US 10,585,731 (reported as a KVM with blue-screen detection; snippet only) | A failure-frames counter increments on successive failed frames and a comparator raises a fault alert past a threshold. | Alerting only; no stronger-model escalation; not an inference loop. Listed as must-pull M7. |
| US 12,567,270 B1 (Squint, above) | A vision-language re-prompt loop stops after a threshold number of iterations; backup tools are used when the default is unavailable or slow. | An iteration cap within one verification, not a cross-frame consecutive-unreadable streak; the fallback is availability-driven, not capability-driven. |
| Practitioner guidance on circuit breakers for language-model APIs (2026; snippet only) | Hysteresis with separate open and close thresholds and minimum open durations to stop a breaker flapping. | Provider-availability breakers, not a readability signal gating a capability upgrade. |

Novelty delta: consecutive-failure counters with reset and cost-driven model cascades are each well known, and a practitioner could plausibly combine them, so this theme is the most exposed. The defensible delta is narrow and claim 10 recites it precisely: the unreadable signal is defined as the navigation record identifying neither an instruction nor an observation; the stronger model is withheld (not spent) and no escalation event is recorded while the per-session consecutive count is below the tolerance; escalation is permitted at the tolerance; a readable record resets; and a user-requested deep read escalates regardless. Specification paragraph [0111] states the technical reason the rule is specific to screen-sampled guidance of a human performer (the correct guidance within the tolerance is the instruction already presented). Risk grade: high; kept as a dependent of claim 1 only.

## 8. Candid summary

Theme 2 is the most crowded and now has a 2026 granted patent on vision-verified guided steps; the anchor claims must continue to rest on the redacted-frame pipeline and the tenant policy. Theme 6 is a likely-obvious combination unless claimed on the precise signal and withholding policy, which claim 10 does. Themes 1, 3, 4 and 5 each have no single anticipating reference found but sit on mature components, so their strength is in the claimed combinations tied to the human task map, its done-states and the frame-verified loop. The primary new independent claim (claim 22) is therefore moderate in strength: it should be expected to survive only as the full combination, and the practitioner should be ready to add the breakdown-gate limitations of claim 23 or the done-state verification of claim 25 into the independent claim if the examiner combines the State Farm and ADaPT or ASAPP references.
